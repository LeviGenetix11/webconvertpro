# Humour

Dry, not jokey. The target reader is a contractor on a lunch break, not a comedy audience. Humour's job here is to make copy survivable to read, not to be the point.

## The one bit we already own
The before/after website comparison on the homepage (`src/index.njk`, `.mock-old` block) is the template for our humour: gently roast the outdated small-business website cliches everyone has seen, never roast the business owner.

Material that's fair game, in that voice:
- Visitor counters ("004127 visitors since 2011")
- "Email form under construction"
- Stock photos of people in suits shaking hands over a laptop that has nothing to do with the trade
- "Welcome to our website!!" as a headline
- A Facebook page that hasn't posted since a provincial election ago
- Phone numbers buried at the bottom of a page nobody scrolls to

Material that is never fair game: the reader's trade, their current website if they built it themselves, their photography, their price point, their town.

## How to use it
- One dry aside per page, max. This isn't a comedy site.
- The joke sets up a real point and gets out of the way — it's not a detour.
- Deadpan delivery: state the absurd thing plainly, don't add "lol" or explain the joke.
- No puns on trade names (no "let's get to the bottom of your plumbing problem"). That's the AI-slop tell for trade content, and it reads as try-hard here too.
- If a sentence needs an exclamation mark to land as funny, it isn't funny. Cut it or rewrite flat.

## Where it shows up
Homepage hero and stats section already use this (the before/after mock, "Websites that work as hard as you do"). Blog posts can open with one dry line about the problem before answering it directly. Service pages should mostly skip humour — those are closer to the moment of "take my money," and a joke there is friction, not charm.
