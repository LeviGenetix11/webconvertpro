How long does it take to build a website? A fast site built on a layout already proven for your trade can go live the next business day after you approve it. A fully custom design built from scratch typically takes 2 to 3 weeks. Both are real options; which one makes sense depends on whether you need something live this week or a brand built specifically around you.

![Clock and calendar representing a website build timeline](/assets/img/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-build-a-website/clock-calendar.jpg)

## Why the timeline varies so much

A DIY builder site might take you a weekend of your own time. A proven-layout build, where the structure and page flow are already tested for your trade, can go live the next business day because the slow parts are already done before you ever send your details. A fully custom design starts from a blank page: brand, layout and copy all built from scratch, which is real work that reasonably takes a couple of weeks, not a shortcut anyone should try to compress.

## What actually slows a website down

The biggest bottleneck on almost every website project isn't the building, it's waiting. Specifically, waiting on the client for photos, logo files, a service list, or a decision on which draft to approve. A designer can build fast; a project still moves at the speed of whoever has to send the last photo or sign off on the final page.

![Small business owner gathering photos and files for a website](/assets/img/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-build-a-website/gathering-files.jpg)

## How this actually breaks down for a home services business

**A proven-layout build (Starter):** send your business name, trade and city, and a working draft is ready within one business day. Once you approve it on a short call, it goes live the next business day. Total time from first contact to live site: often under a week, most of it spent waiting on you, not on the build.

**A custom build (Growth):** designed from scratch around your brand, with pages for each service and city you serve. Plan on 2 to 3 weeks from your approval call, with a full preview link to check on your own phone before it launches.

Neither timeline is padded for effect, they reflect what each approach actually involves.

## What to have ready if you want it faster

- Your business name, the services you offer, and the cities you serve
- A logo, if you have one (if not, this gets built as part of the process)
- Photos of your actual work, phone photos are fine
- Any Google reviews you want pulled onto the site

Showing up with these ready is the single biggest thing you personally control over how fast your own site goes live.
