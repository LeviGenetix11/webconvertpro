The fastest way to get more Google reviews is to ask at the right moment, with a one-tap link, every single time, not as an occasional campaign. Service businesses that treat review requests as a routine step after every job consistently out-rank and out-convert the ones that ask only when they remember to.

![Customer leaving a Google review on a smartphone](/assets/img/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/leaving-review-phone.jpg)

## 1. Ask at the moment the job is actually finished

The best time to ask is right after the work is done and the customer is satisfied, not a week later in a batch email they've already half-forgotten. If you do one job at a time, build the ask into your own closing routine: hand over the invoice, ask for the review, in that order, every time.

## 2. Get a one-tap link and use it everywhere

Google Business Profile gives you a short link that takes a customer straight to the review box, no searching for your listing required. Put that link in your invoice footer, your email signature, and a text message template you can send from the job site in ten seconds.

## 3. Make it routine, not a campaign

A one-time push gets a short spike and then nothing. Asking after every job, every time, builds a steady stream instead, which also keeps your reviews looking current rather than clustered from one push two years ago.

![QR code being scanned with a smartphone](/assets/img/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/qr-code-scan.jpg)

## 4. Put a QR code somewhere physical

Turn your review link into a QR code and put it on the invoice, the van, or a yard sign at a finished job. Someone standing in front of your actual work is exactly the moment a QR code converts best.

## 5. Reply to every review, good and bad

A thoughtful reply to a bad review often does more for a future customer's confidence than another five-star one does, because it shows there's an actual person handling the business, not just collecting stars. Reply to the good ones too; an active review thread reads as a business that's paying attention.

## 6. Never buy or fake them

Fake reviews break Google's rules and can get a profile suspended entirely, and customers can usually tell anyway. If you're short on reviews right now, lead with something true instead, a clear guarantee or a straightforward price, while the real review system builds up.

![Five-star reviews displayed for a local business](/assets/img/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/five-star-reviews.jpg)

## 7. Keep asking after the busy season, not just during it

Review requests tend to happen when a business is thinking about marketing and stop when things get busy, which is backwards: busy season is when you have the most finished jobs to ask about. Build the ask into the job itself so it doesn't depend on remembering.
