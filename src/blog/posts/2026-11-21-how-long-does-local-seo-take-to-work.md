How long does local SEO take to work? Most local SEO work shows its first small movements within 4 to 8 weeks: a keyword creeping onto page two, your Google Business Profile starting to show up for an easier search. Real results, more calls, more map-pack visibility, more of the right people finding you before a competitor, usually take 3 to 6 months of consistent work. A brand-new business with no history yet should plan on 6 to 9 months. If someone promises results in 30 days, they're either overselling or measuring something other than rankings.

![Calendar and growth chart on a desk](/assets/img/blog/how-long-does-local-seo-take-to-work/timeline-calendar.jpg)

## What "working" actually looks like, month by month

**Weeks 1 to 4.** This is setup, not results: your Google Business Profile gets claimed and fully filled out, your website's basic on-page SEO gets fixed, and your business listing gets started across the directories that matter. You likely won't see movement yet. That's normal.

**Months 2 to 3.** Google starts noticing you exist. Impressions tick up in Search Console, a keyword or two moves onto page two, and your profile may start appearing for easier, lower-competition searches.

**Months 3 to 6.** This is where most service businesses start feeling it: calls, direction requests, map-pack visibility for your actual service-plus-city searches. Reviews and citations compound from here, each one making the next one count for a little more.

**Months 6 to 12.** For competitive markets or crowded trades, this is when you're genuinely fighting for the top three map-pack spots, not just appearing somewhere on the page.

![Small business owner checking search rankings on a laptop](/assets/img/blog/how-long-does-local-seo-take-to-work/checking-rankings.jpg)

## Why it's slower for a brand-new business

An established business already has some history: a profile with age and reviews, a site Google has crawled for a while, maybe a few existing citations. A brand-new business is starting that trust from zero on every front at once. Six to nine months is the realistic range, not a sign that something's broken.

## What actually speeds it up (and what doesn't)

Consistency beats intensity. A profile posted to every week and a review asked for after every job, done for six months straight, outperforms a single big push followed by silence. If the last thing on your business's Facebook page was an announcement from a provincial election ago, that's the exact pattern local SEO is working against.

What actually moves the timeline:
- Accurate categories, services and hours set correctly from day one, not fixed later
- A steady request for reviews after every job, not an occasional campaign
- Citations (Google, Bing Places, Apple Maps, Yelp) that match your website exactly, name, address, phone
- Regular profile posts and fresh photos, not a profile claimed once and forgotten

What doesn't help much: more keywords stuffed into your homepage, a bigger one-time push with no follow-up, or switching strategies every few weeks before the last one had time to work.

## Signs it's working before the phone starts ringing

- Search impressions rising in Google Search Console, even before clicks do
- Your Google Business Profile's insights showing more "discovery" searches (people who searched your service, not your name)
- Showing up for the easier, lower-competition version of your search before the competitive one

## What happens if you stop

Local SEO isn't a one-time setup like launching a website. It's maintained, which is the whole reason it's billed monthly instead of once. Stop the posting, the review requests and the citation upkeep, and the ranking you built tends to drift back down over a few months as competitors who kept going pass you.
