How do you choose a web designer? Check their real client sites (not just mockups), ask how fast you'll actually go live, find out whether you pay before or after you see a draft, and confirm they understand your trade specifically, not just "small business" in general. Get all four answers before you look at price.

![Business owner comparing web design options on a laptop](/assets/img/blog/how-to-choose-a-web-designer/comparing-options.jpg)

## Ask to see real client sites, not a portfolio of mockups

A portfolio shows what a designer can design. It doesn't show what you'll actually get. Ask for links to live sites they built for real clients, open them on your own phone, and check whether they still look and load well months after launch. A polished mockup that never shipped tells you nothing about how the designer handles an actual project.

## Ask how fast you'll actually go live

"Fast" means different things to different designers. Get a specific answer: is it days, weeks, or "we'll see"? A proven layout built for your trade can go live in a day. A fully custom design reasonably takes two to three weeks. Either can be the right choice, but you should know which one you're buying before you commit, not after.

![Calendar showing a website launch date](/assets/img/blog/how-to-choose-a-web-designer/launch-timeline.jpg)

## Find out whether you pay before or after you see it

Some designers want a deposit before they've shown you anything. Others build a draft first and let you see it before any money changes hands. Neither approach is a scam, but you should know which one you're agreeing to. If a designer won't show you anything until you've paid, that's worth factoring into the decision, not ignoring.

## Make sure they understand your trade specifically

"We build websites for small businesses" could mean anything. A designer who's actually built for home services businesses before, contractors, roofers, plumbers, electricians, HVAC, landscapers, knows that your customer is searching "emergency plumber near me" at 11pm, not browsing a catalog. That shows up in where the phone number sits, how the quote form is worded, and what gets featured on the homepage.

![Contractor reviewing a quote request on a tablet](/assets/img/blog/how-to-choose-a-web-designer/trade-specific-site.jpg)

## Ask whether local SEO is included or a separate add-on

A website that looks good but never shows up in local search isn't doing its job. Ask directly: does the price include basic on-page SEO, or is ranking work billed separately? Both are legitimate, but "included" and "extra" change what you're actually comparing when you look at two quotes side by side.

## Watch for exclusivity, or the lack of it

If a designer builds the same layout for every roofer in your city, you're not getting a competitive advantage, you're getting the same site as the next guy with your logo swapped in. Ask whether they'll take on a direct competitor in your same service area right after you. A designer who limits that is protecting your investment; one who doesn't think about it probably isn't thinking about your results either.

## A short checklist before you commit

1. Can I see real client sites, not mockups?
2. What's the actual turnaround time, in days or weeks?
3. Do I pay before or after I see a draft?
4. Have they built for my specific trade before?
5. Is local SEO included, or priced separately?
6. Will they take on my direct competitor next?

If a designer gives you a straight answer to all six without dodging, that's a good sign on its own.
